# Pretrained backbones and foundation models for WSP radiology setups

**Snapshot:** 2026-11-12. This is the pretrained-model shard for settings S1–S56, not a dataset inventory. “Open weights” does not mean OSI-open or commercial-use rights: check each checkpoint's license, upstream base-model license, and training-data terms before use. Dates below mean first publicly evidenced code/checkpoint/paper availability; exact checkpoint day is marked uncertain where it cannot be established. **Bold `POST-CUTOFF`** means public checkpoint availability after 2026-07-01.

**Tool note:** the common brief required `exa_search` and prohibited `web_search`, but no `exa_search` tool was exposed. Per supervisor instruction, I used the available `web_search` with `provider="exa"`, plus direct `fetch_content` on official/model-card sources. Exa's free endpoint rate-limited some follow-up queries; claims prioritize fetched primary sources and uncertainty is explicit.

## Summary

Most practical 24-GB-GPU starts are DINOv3 ViT-B/16 (natural-image control), RAD-DINO (CXR specialist), BiomedCLIP/MedSigLIP (medical image-text), MedGemma 4B (generalist medical VLM), MAIRA-2 (CXR reports), and MedSAM/MedSAM2 or VISTA3D (segmentation). Strong fresh post-cutoff options are **NeuroVFM, nnFoundation, NV-Reason-CT, and UltraSAM3**; these support version-upgrade, 3D, and ultrasound comparisons, but vary in license clarity, pretraining overlap, and 24-GB feasibility.

SAEs have been applied to MedCLIP, Mammo-CLIP, radiology report VLMs, segmentation networks, and DINOv3/BiomedParse. X-Edit (MICCAI 2026) directly updates medical ViT weights; Med-LEGO is prior weight/LoRA composition work; M³Bench studies model editing in medical VLMs. Most SAE “steering” papers intervene on activations at inference, not durable weights. Treat those as useful feature-discovery/control baselines, not proof that permanent WSP edits work.

## Conventions

- **24 GB** is a practical estimate, not a published benchmark: one 24-GB GPU, mixed precision, batch 1 where needed, checkpointing, ordinary preprocessing. `Yes` = standard full tuning of smaller variant plausible; `LoRA/adapter` = full tuning is not a safe assumption; `Uncertain/no` = profile exact recipe. Full-volume 3D and high-resolution mammography can be activation-memory limited even when parameter counts are modest.
- The **pretraining data** column is intended to enable contamination/overlap audits. Missing or partial disclosures are uncertainty, not evidence of no overlap.
- **Interpretability** distinguishes an available model-specific SAE from generic hooks/probes or a paper whose artifacts may not be released.

**Practical tooling:** MedSAE repo; Universal Boosts causal-feature/dashboard artifacts; Med-SegLens; PyTorch forward hooks and HF hidden states; generic SAE libraries as components. Vision/3D models need custom activation capture, dictionary training, reconstruction/faithfulness and causal ablation tests. SAELens/TransformerLens-like LLM tooling is not a drop-in clinically validated solution for every visual tower. Pair each edit with random-feature/direction controls, per-edit revert ablations and an explicit diff log.

## G. Priorities and residual risks

1. **First pilot:** DINOv3-B vs RAD-DINO for S14, with a truly unseen target site, strong LP-FT/LoRA/continued-SSL baselines, power sized test sets and known overlap exclusions.
2. **Positive control:** DINOv3 HU-window circuit in S15 or input inversion in S12. This verifies that the edit machinery can implement a known transform before broader mechanistic claims.
3. **Best version migration:** DINOv2-B→DINOv3-B under S45 with 0/5/25 labels and unlabeled paired calibration; distinguish alignment-map effect from any fine-tuning.
4. **Interpreter-backed specialty:** Mammo-CLIP/Mammo-SAE (S21/S4) and MedCLIP/MedSAE or report-VLM SAE methods (S17/S35). Require clinician validation of SAE labels and causal interventions.
5. **Post-cutoff leads:** UltraSAM3 for US (S16), NeuroVFM/nnFoundation for 3D CT/MRI (S6/S56), NV-Reason-CT for native-3D CT reasoning. Freshness is useful, but overlap, 3D memory and license restrictions can dominate.

**Residual risks:** (i) Exact first public checkpoint dates remain uncertain for some older models (RAD-DINO, Merlin, MAE, ConvNeXt V2) and a few community/3D weights have unclear separate licenses. (ii) Recheck terms at download/use time; code, model card, weight, data and upstream base may differ. (iii) A post-cutoff checkpoint is not enough: date the actual test set and check if its examples were in old pretraining data. (iv) Overlap statements are screening alerts, not substitutes for patient-level and near-duplicate audits. (v) 24-GB recommendations are estimates; profile exact crop, optimizer and trainable modules before preregistration.
